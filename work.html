Program Management

Building a Finance PMO from Zero

Context Joined a fast-growing international group where the Finance function had no dedicated project management structure. Multiple initiatives were running in parallel with no visibility, no governance, and no consistent reporting cadence.
Approach Assessed the full landscape of active Finance initiatives. Introduced a project intake process, standardised documentation templates, and established a weekly governance rhythm across all Finance leads. Built out a Confluence-based programme hub covering charters, RACI matrices, risk logs, and status reporting. Gradually expanded scope from Finance into cross-functional programs touching Legal, Operations, and Technology.
Outcome
Within six months, all Finance initiatives were tracked, owned, and reported against. Portfolio visibility improved from near-zero to full weekly executive reporting. The framework was later adopted as the basis for the wider PMO governance model.

From no PM focus to all functions driving their own projects with discipline and transparency.

Process Design

Designing a Company-wide Project Intake Process

Context Project requests were arriving informally, often bypassing prioritisation and resource planning entirely. The PMO had no structured way to evaluate, triage, or schedule incoming work, leading to reactive delivery and poor resource allocation.
Approach Co-designed a Project Intake Form covering business case, strategic alignment, estimated effort, dependencies, and stakeholder sign-off requirements. Established a PMO Committee triage process where requests are reviewed, scored, and prioritised against the portfolio. Rolled the process out across all business units and embedded it into the standard project lifecycle.
Outcome
Intake process became the single entry point for all new project requests. Reduced reactive project starts, improved stakeholder alignment at the point of initiation, and gave leadership a clear view of the incoming pipeline at any point in time.

A repeatable, scalable intake mechanism that replaced informal requests with structured governance.

Delivery

Cross-functional Program Delivery in IT

Context Tasked with leading 10+ cross-functional IT projects simultaneously, spanning engineering, product, security, and infrastructure teams. Each project had different stakeholders, timelines, and risk profiles. Delivery predictability was low and escalations were frequent.
Approach Implemented a consistent SDLC framework across all projects, standardising planning, execution, UAT, and release stages. Introduced Jira-based tracking with custom JQL dashboards giving real-time visibility per workstream. Established a structured escalation path and regular stakeholder reporting cadence. Co-led the PMO process rollout to ensure consistency across teams.
Outcome
All 10+ projects delivered on scope, budget, and timeline. Delivery predictability improved significantly quarter-on-quarter. The SDLC framework was adopted as a company standard, reducing rework cycles and improving inter-team coordination.

10+ projects. Zero misses on scope, budget, or timeline.

Documentation Standards

Project Charter

Defines the project before execution begins. Serves as the single source of truth for scope, ownership, and success criteria.

  • Project overview and strategic rationale
  • Objectives and measurable success criteria
  • Scope definition: in scope, out of scope, assumptions
  • Key stakeholders and RACI summary
  • High-level timeline and milestone map
  • Budget overview and resource allocation
  • Key risks and initial mitigation approach
  • Approval and sign-off section

Business Requirements Document (BRD)

Captures what the business needs from a solution before technical design begins. Bridges business stakeholders and delivery teams.

  • Business context and problem statement
  • Functional requirements by business area
  • Non-functional requirements (performance, security, compliance)
  • Workflow diagrams and process maps
  • RACI matrix for requirement ownership
  • Acceptance criteria per requirement
  • Open questions and decision log

RACI Matrix

Clarifies decision-making authority and accountability across workstreams. Prevents ambiguity at key delivery moments.

  • Activity or deliverable listed per row
  • Stakeholders and roles listed per column
  • R (Responsible), A (Accountable), C (Consulted), I (Informed) per cell
  • Single Accountable owner enforced per activity
  • Reviewed and agreed at project kickoff
  • Updated at each phase gate

Executive Status Report

Provides senior leadership with a concise, accurate view of programme health without requiring them to dig into operational detail.

  • Overall RAG status (Red / Amber / Green) with rationale
  • Summary of progress since last report
  • Key milestones: achieved, upcoming, at risk
  • Active risks and issues with owner and mitigation
  • Decisions required from leadership
  • OKR or KR progress tracking where applicable
  • Next period priorities

Risk & Issue Log

Maintains a living record of threats to delivery. Ensures risks are surfaced early, owned, and mitigated before they become blockers.

  • Unique ID and date raised
  • Description and category (technical, resource, dependency, etc.)
  • Probability and impact scoring
  • Overall risk rating
  • Owner and mitigation/resolution plan
  • Status and target resolution date
  • Escalation flag if required

Process Frameworks

Project Delivery Lifecycle

The end-to-end framework applied consistently across programs to ensure predictable, quality delivery.

01

Intake & Triage

Request submitted via Project Intake Form. PMO Committee reviews against strategic priorities, capacity, and dependencies. Decision: approve, defer, or decline.

02

Initiation

Project Charter drafted and signed off. RACI established. Kickoff meeting held with all key stakeholders. Confluence project space created with full governance structure.

03

Requirements & Planning

BRD produced through structured stakeholder sessions. Project plan built with milestones, dependencies, and resource allocation. Risk log populated and baselined.

04

Execution

Delivery managed via Jira with sprint or milestone tracking. Weekly status updates issued. Risks and issues reviewed and actioned continuously. Stakeholder communication maintained throughout.

05

Review & Closure

Delivery validated against acceptance criteria. Lessons learned documented. Project formally closed in Jira and Confluence. Handover to operational owner completed.

Programme Reporting Cadence

Reporting rhythm applied across all active programs to maintain visibility without creating overhead.

Daily

Team Standups

Blockers surfaced and resolved within 24 hours. Status updated in Jira. Automated weekly digest sent to stakeholders.

Weekly

Programme Status Report

RAG status per project. Milestone progress. Risks and escalations. Published to all stakeholders and stored in Confluence.

Monthly

Executive Summary

Portfolio health overview for senior leadership. OKR tracking. Decisions required. Strategic alignment review.

Quarterly

Portfolio Review

Full review of active and planned programs. Resource reallocation. Prioritisation against updated business objectives. Intake pipeline review.
